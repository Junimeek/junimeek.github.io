Juniper's Commentary on the VIA Better Bus Plan

This page was last updated on August 21, 2026.

This page is an opinion piece about VIA Metropolitan Transit's Better Bus plan, featuring hot takes, snark, and some cynicism.

Photo by Junimeek

The current state of Better Bus

The VIA Better Bus plan is something that several years ago I personally would not have thought we would ever need. Aside from frequencies being extremely low (especially when compared to what they were before the pandemic hit), it was a perfectly fine system, especially for being severely underfunded. However, now that major changes are happening system-wide, I would say that it has been mostly positive changes: we're getting a real BRT line, frequencies are getting boosted to 30 minutes system-wide, and making transfers is overall easier than before.

One change that I personally take advantage of is one of the first changes that were made: the reintroduction of Route 10. The alignment that gets designated with the number 10 has a bit of a strange history: archives of a timetable from before the Comprehensive Service Plan of 2003 (CSP) was implemented show that it was a limited-stop service that is similar to today's 10 with two major differences: it followed the alignment of today's 9, and it had an extension to provide rush hour service to the Rolling Oaks Mall in the northeast side. Over time, it would be merged into the 9 and split off again multiple times, and today it has once again been reintroduced as a Skip Service, giving Broadway a significant boost in the number of buses that operate along it, and giving the Naco Pass transfer hub more departures bound for Downtown.

Aside from that though, there haven't been many changes that have benefited me specifically. But that's okay: the point of Better Bus isn't to benefit just a few people, it's to benefit as many San Antonians as possible. That's the point of public transportation after all.

I'm also a huge fan of the Green Line. While it sucks that we won't be getting a proper light rail line that so many of us want, it's difficult to get around the fact that VIA is underfunded, so the impulse to pursue BRT instead of LRT is understandable. Though, I do question why VIA insists on referring to the Green Line as "Advanced Rapid Transit". I suppose it's possible that it's simply to avoid any possible confusion with the existing Prímo system, but my cynical take is that they're trying to hide the fact that Prímo was originally advertised as a BRT network before quietly dropping that designation in the late 2010s. Kind of strange, but oh well, it's not something that's worth dwelling on.

However, what I think is worth dwelling on is my single biggest problem with the Keep San Antonio Moving program as a whole: VIA Link expansion.
